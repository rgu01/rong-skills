# Workplace AI Policy: Baseline Survey of Named Employers

A baseline survey compiled on 2026-08-11 for the `AI at Work` section of the
[creating-ai-newsletters](../../skills/ai/creating-ai-newsletters/SKILL.md) skill.

The newsletter section only publishes a stance **change** that falls inside a
one-week coverage window. That rule is only usable if there is a record of what
each employer's stance already was. This file is that record. It deliberately
ignores the date window and reaches back to the first bans in 2023.

## How to use this file

1. Before writing an `AI at Work` section, read this file to see whether a
   candidate is a genuine change or a restatement of a stance already recorded
   here.
2. After publishing an `AI at Work` story, add or update the organization's row
   here with the new stance, the exact date, and the edition that carried it.
3. Never promote a row marked **Single source** or **Unverified** into an
   edition without first confirming it against independent reporting. The
   newsletter's source rules still apply; this file is a research ledger, not a
   set of publishable claims.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Verified** — primary source, or two independent non-government sources.
- **Partial** — the event is confirmed but a material detail could not be read.
- **Single source** — one source only, often a vendor-authored compilation.
- **Unverified** — source independence or the date could not be established.

## Stance taxonomy

The same three-way split the skill uses, judged by what the organization does to
its own workforce rather than by what it says about AI in general:

- **Encouraging** — mandating, funding, licensing, training, incentivizing, or
  measuring AI use.
- **Discouraging** — restricting AI to approved tools, teams, data classes, or
  tasks; metering spend; requiring disclosure or human review.
- **Disallowing** — prohibiting AI tools outright for employees or for a named
  function, unit, or data class.

## Employer AI applied to employees

Adjacent to the section's scope: the employer is not governing employee use of
AI but running AI on the workforce. Worth tracking because it drives the same
internal politics.

| Organization | Date | Event | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta (Model Capability Initiative) | launched April 2026, paused June 2026 | Mandatory for most US staff, collecting keystrokes, mouse activity, occasional screenshots, performance data and internal conversations as training data. Paused after that data became readable company-wide, classed internally as SEV 2 | Verified (Business Insider, 2026-06-22) |

## Out of scope: contributor policies

These govern contributors rather than employees, so the `AI at Work` section
excludes them. They are the same policy question one step out, and belong in
`Other AI Stories` when they qualify.

- Codeberg: members voted 358–144, vote closed 2026-07-22, to stop hosting
  projects consisting mostly of AI-generated code, citing CI and storage cost.
- GCC: rejects AI-generated contributions over 15 lines on GPL copyright
  grounds (reported 2026-07-31).
- Zig, NetBSD, GIMP, Gentoo, qemu, Flathub: reject LLM-generated pull requests.

## Out of scope: aggregate and regulatory framing

Excluded from the section by rule — no named organization changing its own
stance — but useful for an executive brief.

- Almost every Fortune 500 tracks overall AI usage (2026-05-05); token billing
  makes every prompt a trackable expense, so employers see cost clearly and
  effectiveness poorly.
- 54% of workers bypassed their employer's AI tools in the previous 30 days, and
  roughly eight in ten enterprise workers avoid or actively reject deployed
  tools.
- An April 2026 survey found 29% of employees admit sabotaging their company's
  AI strategy, rising to 44% among Gen Z.
- Nine in ten organizations claim an AI policy while 70% of IT leaders have
  caught staff using unapproved AI (reported 2026-08-10).
- The UK consulted until 2026-09-30 on requiring employers to consult workers
  before deploying monitoring software, including AI productivity scoring
  (reported 2026-08-04). Sector-wide regulation, excluded on two counts.

## The arc so far

The 2023 wave was security-driven prohibition, aimed at stopping confidential
data from reaching a public chatbot. 2025 flipped to mandates wired into reviews,
promotions, and headcount requests. 2026 is the correction: Duolingo dropped its
evaluation requirement, Uber metered spend after blowing a budget in four months,
Meta paused its workforce-monitoring programme after leaking the data it
collected, and HubSpot's no-mandate result became the case study cited against
mandates. Encouragement now competes with cost control and worker refusal rather
than with security alone.

## Coverage gaps

Worth targeted searching in future editions, since none of it surfaced here:

- Non-US, non-UK employers outside financial services and Big Tech.
- Public-sector employers other than the UAE and the US House, where the
  two-independent-source rule applies.
- Unions bargaining AI clauses, which set an employer stance by agreement rather
  than by memo.
- Employers reversing a *ban* into permitted use through an approved internal
  tool, which is the mirror image of the 2026 reversals recorded above.
