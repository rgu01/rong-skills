# Pedestrian-Crossing Assignment Package Design

## Purpose

Create a standalone assignment package for the lecture "Formal Modeling and
Analysis of Real-Time Systems" in Embedded Systems II. The assignment must let
an individual student demonstrate, within two to three hours, that they can:

- interpret an informal real-time-system requirement;
- build and analyse a network of timed automata in UPPAAL;
- connect verification queries and results back to system requirements;
- diagnose a failed verification run and repair its cause; and
- use artificial intelligence as an assistant without treating its output as
  evidence of correctness.

The assignment is assessed pass/revision. Teachers normally grade from the
student's report alone. They may open and run the submitted UPPAAL XML model to
resolve inconsistencies or spot-check reported results.

## Design Principles

### Evidence over prose

The report is a structured evidence record rather than a free-form essay.
Polished prose, including prose generated by artificial intelligence, earns no
credit on its own. A passing report must show a consistent chain:

> understood requirement -> justified interpretation -> corresponding model
> -> appropriate property -> genuine UPPAAL result -> correct interpretation

### Guided freedom

The core crossing behavior and mandatory checks are fixed so that every
submission has comparable substance. Students retain judgment in two places:

1. choosing and defending a small number of timing parameters; and
2. designing one additional timed extension and its property.

This avoids both extremes: a trivial generated model and an open-ended project
that cannot be completed in one short assignment.

### Verification and validation

UPPAAL verifies properties of the submitted model. The student remains
responsible for validating that the model and properties represent the stated
intent. The report template therefore asks for both tool results and their
engineering meaning.

## Package Structure

The package will live at:

```text
experiments/Course-Embedded-System-II/pedestrian-crossing-assignment/
├── student/
│   ├── assignment.md
│   └── report-template.md
└── teacher/
    ├── grading-guide.md
    ├── checking-points.md
    ├── pass-criteria.md
    └── expected-solution-shape.md
```

Only the two files under `student/` are distributed to students. Files under
`teacher/` contain assessment guidance and must not be distributed.

## Student Assignment

### Scenario

The student models a signalised pedestrian crossing as an embedded real-time
controller interacting with an environment. The system includes vehicle
traffic lights, pedestrian WALK/DON'T-WALK indications, a pedestrian request
button, a minimum vehicle-green interval, an all-red clearance interval, and a
WALK interval.

The assignment supplies fixed physical or policy facts where unrestricted
choice would consume time without demonstrating formal-methods knowledge:

- one time unit is one second;
- the crossing is 6 metres wide;
- the design pedestrian speed is 1 metre per second;
- allow 1 second before the pedestrian starts moving;
- the minimum uninterrupted vehicle-green interval is 10 seconds; and
- the all-red clearance interval is 2 seconds before enabling the conflicting
  movement.

The student chooses and justifies only:

- `WALK_TIME`, between 7 and 12 seconds, using the supplied crossing context;
  and
- `MAX_WAIT`, between 14 and 30 seconds, measured from the request event to
  entry into WALK.

The 14-second lower bound accounts for the least-favourable request point:
2 seconds of clearance, 10 seconds of minimum vehicle green, and 2 seconds of
clearance before WALK begins.

The assignment will use integer time units and state all supplied timing facts
in one table. Chosen values must be feasible and used consistently throughout
the report, model, and queries.

### Mandatory modeled behavior

The submitted model must represent:

- an environment capable of issuing a pedestrian request whenever WALK is
  inactive;
- a controller with vehicle, clearance, and pedestrian phases;
- preservation of a request until it is served, with repeated presses while a
  request is pending merged into that pending request without resetting its
  wait measurement;
- entry into WALK only in response to a pending request, with that entry
  serving and clearing the request;
- a minimum vehicle-green duration before changing phase;
- an all-red clearance interval of exactly 2 seconds after either movement is
  stopped and before the conflicting movement is enabled;
- a WALK phase that lasts for exactly the chosen `WALK_TIME`; and
- return to normal vehicle operation after serving the request.

The design must contain at least two interacting automata: a controller and an
environment/button component. A student may add an observer automaton for
bounded-time verification. The assignment will not prescribe exact locations
or synchronisation mechanisms.

### Mandatory verification evidence

The report must include exact embedded UPPAAL queries and results for:

1. reachability of the WALK phase;
2. absence of simultaneous vehicle green and pedestrian WALK;
3. universal eventual service at WALK entry, no later than the inclusive
   `MAX_WAIT` boundary, for every request issued while WALK is inactive;
4. absence of deadlock; and
5. at least one non-vacuity or supporting reachability check needed to make the
   preceding results meaningful.

For bounded response, students may use separate universal eventual-service and
numeric-deadline evidence, or a sound combined construction. An optional
monitor/observer is judged relative to its query: the checked property must
detect every unserved or late request while allowing service exactly at
`MAX_WAIT`. The assignment will explain the required outcome without supplying
a complete monitor.

### Required failed run and repair

Every student must document one genuine failed verification run encountered or
created during development. It may concern a core requirement or the
student-designed timed extension. If the student's first design passes, they
must make one controlled, relevant change, predict its effect, and run UPPAAL
to obtain a real counterexample.

The report records:

- the deliberate or accidental change;
- the expected and actual query result;
- a genuine UPPAAL diagnostic trace record tied to the failed model/run and
  version, using pasted output when available or a faithful, clearly labelled
  transcription of the relevant simulator states and transitions;
- a hand reconstruction of the trace with component locations, delays, clock
  values, synchronisations, and important updates;
- the diagnosed cause;
- the repair; and
- the successful re-verification result.

The trace record stays in `report.md`: no separate export/conversion tool or
third submission file is required. The hand reconstruction remains a distinct
semantic expansion with delays, clock values, updates, and enabling reasons.

This requirement tests semantic understanding rather than the ability to
present a model that happens to pass.

### Student-designed timed extension

The student must add exactly one useful timed extension that changes the model's
behavior and is not a restatement of a core requirement. The extension must:

- have a time-related condition or effect;
- require a concrete model change, not only an additional query;
- be described as an informal requirement;
- be formalised using an invariant/safety, liveness, or reachability property;
- include a non-vacuity check where relevant; and
- be verified and interpreted in the report.

The assignment may mention example categories, such as night mode, an audible
signal, button debouncing, or bounded bus priority, while explicitly forbidding
copying an example without defining its precise behavior.

### Artificial-intelligence use

Artificial-intelligence assistance is allowed and expected. The student must
report one significant suggestion from an AI assistant and state:

- what the assistant suggested;
- which assumption or requirement it was intended to address;
- what the student checked independently;
- whether the suggestion was accepted, modified, or rejected; and
- which UPPAAL evidence informed that decision.

A transcript is not required. The assessed skill is critical use of the
assistant, not prompt style or quantity of AI use.

### Submission and workload

The student submits:

- one final runnable UPPAAL `.xml` model with queries embedded; and
- one completed Markdown report generated from the supplied template, renamed
  `report.md`.

The report target is concise: approximately three pages plus the hand-trace
table if necessary. The model, verification work, and report are scoped for an
individual two-to-three-hour assignment.

## Report Template

The report template will contain fixed headings and tables for:

1. student and tool information;
2. requirement interpretation and timing constants;
3. model architecture and component responsibilities;
4. a requirement-to-query traceability matrix with expected result, actual
   result, and engineering interpretation;
5. failed verification, counterexample, hand trace, diagnosis, and repair;
6. the student-designed timed extension;
7. one significant AI-assisted decision;
8. final consistency checks and submission declaration.

Prompts will request exact names, formulas, states, and clock values. Generic
claims such as "the property passed" will not satisfy a field that asks for the
query, result, and interpretation.

## Teacher Assessment

### Grading method

Teachers first read the report without opening the model. They assess whether
the evidence is complete, technically plausible, mutually consistent, and
connected to the informal requirements. The model is run only as an optional
spot-check or when a claim cannot be resolved from the report.

The grading guide will emphasise causal explanation and traceability, not the
beauty of the automata or the fluency of the prose.

### Checking points

The checking-points file will follow report order so a teacher can mark it in a
single pass. It will cover:

- explicit and feasible timing choices;
- credible environment/controller decomposition;
- preservation of requests;
- correct correspondence between each requirement and query;
- non-vacuity of passing properties;
- internally consistent reported UPPAAL outcomes;
- a possible and correctly interpreted counterexample trace;
- a repair that addresses the diagnosed cause;
- exactly one genuine timed extension; and
- a specific example of AI output being checked rather than trusted.

### Pass boundary

A submission passes only when all essential learning outcomes are demonstrated:

1. **Requirements:** interpretations and timing decisions are explicit,
   feasible, and consistently used.
2. **Model:** the described model could implement the intended crossing and
   contains the mandatory behavior.
3. **Properties:** the mandatory queries match the requirements and are not
   merely vacuous.
4. **Evidence:** results are concrete and plausibly produced by UPPAAL.
5. **Diagnosis:** the student correctly connects one failing trace to a model or
   requirement defect and explains an effective repair.
6. **Extension:** the timed extension changes behavior and is meaningfully
   verified.
7. **AI judgment:** the student shows a specific assistant contribution and an
   independent formal or semantic check of it.

Minor modelling, notation, or presentation defects may pass when they do not
undermine these outcomes. A submission requires revision if any essential
outcome is absent or contradicted. Fabricated evidence, an impossible hand
trace, a vacuous property presented as proof, or unexplained inconsistency
between requirements, model, queries, and results cannot pass.

### Expected solution shape

The teacher reference will describe acceptable solution families rather than a
single answer. It will include:

- a minimal credible component decomposition;
- typical phase progression and clock roles;
- suitable UPPAAL query patterns;
- common vacuity traps and modelling errors;
- plausible controlled faults and their symptoms; and
- examples of acceptable and insufficient student-designed timed extensions.

It will not contain a golden XML model. The assessment target is the student's
understanding and evidence chain, not structural similarity to an instructor
automaton.

## Consistency and Quality Checks

Before delivery, the package will be checked for:

- identical terminology and timing facts across all student and teacher files;
- exact correspondence between report fields and teacher checking points;
- exact correspondence between essential checking points and pass criteria;
- no teacher-only hints in student files;
- no requirement that depends on a UPPAAL feature not introduced or explained;
- no task that requires more than the intended two-to-three-hour workload;
- no suggested extension that constitutes a complete ready-made answer; and
- no placeholder text or unresolved design decision.
