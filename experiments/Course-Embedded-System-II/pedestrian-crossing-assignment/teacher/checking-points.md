# Report-order checking points

> **Confidential teacher material. Do not distribute to students.**

Read the report in order and record one outcome per section: **OK** when the section gives coherent evidence, **Concern** when a material doubt needs holistic resolution or an optional Extensible Markup Language (XML) spot-check, and **Missing** when required report evidence is absent. A Concern does not automatically mean revision; resolve it against the complete requirement-to-evidence chain and, only when useful, the final `model.xml`.

| Report section | Checking point | Satisfactory evidence | Concern requiring model spot-check | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Student and tools | Student identity; identifier; exact UPPAAL version; artificial intelligence (AI) assistants and versions, or “None” | Complete, unambiguous tool and authorship record | Tool/version record is partial or conflicts with syntax/results reported later |  |
| 2. Timing and interpretation | One-second time unit; integer `WALK_TIME` in [7, 12] s with 6 m ÷ 1 m/s + 1 s calculation and margin; integer `MAX_WAIT` in [14, 30] s with least-favourable 2 s + 10 s + 2 s rationale; request event; pending state; merged repeats without clock reset; preservation until WALK entry; entry clears pending; exact phase timing and return; longer green preserves bound; request-to-entry measurement; inclusive boundary; timeout mechanism | Values, calculations, semantics, guards/updates, and bound convention form one concrete, internally consistent account | Constant or mechanism conflicts with later query/model names; exact clearance/WALK duration or the inclusive `MAX_WAIT` boundary cannot be established from the report |  |
| 3. Model architecture | Every listed template; responsibility; actual locations/modes; clocks/variables; interactions; optional observer; timed-extension changes | Named environment and controller genuinely interact; every table field names concrete structures with roles consistent with sections 2, 4, and 6 | Names appear invented or inconsistent; requests may not influence control; observer or extension structure is too vague to assess |  |
| 4. Requirement-to-query traceability | R1–R5 each has operational interpretation; exact embedded Timed Computation Tree Logic (TCTL) query; predicted result; exact actual UPPAAL result; conservative engineering meaning; non-vacuity. R1 reaches WALK; R2 universally excludes conflicting greens; R3 proves universal eventual service and service by the inclusive numeric bound, separately or through one sound combined construction; R4 explicitly proves network deadlock freedom without overclaiming; R5 reaches relied-on triggers/behaviours. Observer soundness is judged relative to its query: every relevant late or unserved path must be detected under that formula; service at exactly `MAX_WAIT` must not lose an equality race to timeout; measurement stops after service | All five identifiers occur once; exact queries/results support their stated meanings; R3 covers both universal obligations with reachable request/service behaviour | Query/result is paraphrased or inconsistent; a universal claim may be vacuous; R3 supplies only a clock bound or an existential service path; observer completeness, synchronisation ordering, or equality handling cannot be resolved from the report |  |
| 5. Genuine failed run, diagnosis, and repair | Defect/change and scope; prior prediction; failed-model identifier; trace provenance; exact failing query/result; genuine diagnostic excerpt; hand prefix with locations, delays/actions, clock values, synchronisations/updates, and enabling reasons; causal diagnosis; concrete repair; repaired/final identifier; exact re-query/result; causal explanation and version linkage | Tool excerpt and hand values agree with an identified failed version; the trace demonstrates the violation; the repair addresses its cause; re-verification is tied to the repaired/final version | Trace provenance, clock arithmetic, names, or result is doubtful; a false existential query is presented as a diagnostic counterexample; failed and final evidence may be mixed |  |
| 6. Exactly one student-designed timed extension | Precise useful requirement; timed trigger/guard/bound and effect; concrete model delta; justified property class; exact embedded query; expected and actual result; non-vacuity or concrete reason it is inapplicable; engineering interpretation of changed behaviour | One timed extension changes model behaviour, and its requirement, mechanism, property, result, and non-vacuity evidence align | Only a constant/query/name changed; mechanism is unclear; claimed trigger or outcome may be unreachable; query and stated extension concern different behaviour |  |
| 7. AI-assisted decision | Significant suggestion in the student's words; relevant requirement/assumption; independent semantic or formal check; accept/modify/reject judgment and reason; exact UPPAAL query/result informing the decision | Concrete suggestion is tested independently and the student makes a technically supported judgment | Statement is generic, transcript-like, or treats AI output as verifier evidence; cited query/result cannot be connected to the decision |  |
| 8. Final consistency and responsibility | Constants and ranges; exact queries/results and identified versions; failed trace/version linkage; failed versus repaired/final distinction; all mandatory, extension, and supporting queries embedded; one real timed extension; exactly two submission files; completed declaration | Checklist and declaration are complete and consistent with sections 1–7 and the final model claims | Template markers remain; names, constants, results, or versions conflict; declaration or submission consistency is incomplete |  |
